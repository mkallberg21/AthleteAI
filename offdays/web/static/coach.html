Needs a nudge

When position training starts

Below this age every athlete gets the all-round plan, whatever position is on their jersey. Position is still recorded and still shown, and it just does not narrow what they practise. Early single-role specialisation is the pattern youth sports medicine warns about most consistently, so the default is deliberately late. This is your call, not ours.

Where the season is

These budgets only ever counted work on top of team practice, so the phase changes them. In-season the figure goes down, because your athletes already have practices and games in their week, and stacking a full off-season target on top of that is how a season ends badly. Nothing is inferred from the calendar; sports do not share a season and a wrong guess would quietly change what every child here is told to do.

Time outside practice

Against an age-appropriate weekly budget, not a target to beat. Both ends matter: an athlete well past their budget is a check-in, same as one who has not started. School, friends and other sports are the rest of the week.

How everyone is doing

Side by side

Off by default. Ranking a nine-year-old against their thirteen-year-old sibling by reps says nothing except which of them is older. Turned on, this compares turning up and moving well, things a younger one can actually win, and never reps.

Automatic recognition

Reserved for the long streaks by default. A note from someone like that means something because it does not arrive every week, and putting their name on a three-day streak spends exactly what made it worth having. Leave it blank and everything comes from the athlete's coach.

Skill work

Film study

Short clips, curated by you. Nothing is uploaded or re-hosted. You paste a link and athletes watch it in the provider's own player, which means their browser talks to that provider. Keep clips to the moment that teaches something: they are capped by age, and an athlete's whole day of film is single digits of minutes on purpose.

Carrying something

What athletes have told the app themselves. You see the area, how bad, and how long. Anything they wrote in their own notes goes to them and their parent or guardian, and not here. None of this is a diagnosis, and an athlete flagged here has not lost anything by saying so.

Workload watch

Athletes whose training has jumped sharply or who have not taken a rest day. These are prompts to check in, not diagnoses, and this only counts work logged here, so team practices are on top.

Assignments

Compliance is worst-first, because this list exists to tell you who needs a text tonight.

Evaluation export

For tryouts and end-of-season reviews. It carries participation and improvement and deliberately not volume, because reps and XP mostly measure whether a child has a garage, a wall, and a lift to practice, which is not something they control. It is alphabetical, has no total, and contains nobody's injury history. Weeks an athlete was hurt or away come out of their participation rate without saying why.

A number the squad chases together

Not a leaderboard. A goal here counts how many athletes clear a small personal bar, so the keen one doing six sessions moves it exactly as much as the quiet one doing three, and the only way it goes up is somebody new turning up. You see the total; nobody sees who is in or who is not, including you. The names you need for a nudge are on your roster above.
